Ute Peak is a the highest peak in the Williams Fork Mountains. It is a 12 mile slog to get to the top but you will likely not see a single other person the entire hike.

Ute Peak was named after the Ute people or Parianuche. Ute peak popped up in The name "Ute Peak" began consistently appearing on regional U.S. government geological survey maps and forestry charts in the late 1800s and early 1900s.

Three facts about Ute Peak

  • Ute Peak is 12,303 feet making it the 3rd tallest peak in the Williams Fork Mountains
  • Ute Peak is a popular back country skiing location.
  • Ute Peak sits directly on top of the Continental Divide of the Americas
